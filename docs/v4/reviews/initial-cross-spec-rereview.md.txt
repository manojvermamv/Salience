### I2 — ADDRESSED: authoritative action fencing

**Evidence:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-p1-production-routing-and-worker-fencing-design.md:63`–`:69`, `:76`, `:107`, `:118`; `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-p1-production-policy-and-revocation-design.md:99`–`:101`, `:119`–`:121`, `:171`.

Routing now owns a versioned `RouteFenceAuthority.authorize_action` interface. It checks current owner/epoch, exact target and route/artifact digests, action/operation binding, and lease-service time. Its order is linearizable with claim, renewal, expiry, termination, and transfer. Policy consumes a unique one-use decision in the current policy/stop and action transaction; carrying an epoch or checking only at startup is explicitly insufficient.

The committed-action versus later-action boundary is stated: an already consumed exact claim remains an in-flight liability; a stale process cannot use it to authorize another action. Expiry/transfer invalidates unused decisions. A target that cannot demonstrate this ordering remains HELD. Real-process pause beyond TTL, service outage, and owner termination/transfer are included in acceptance at issue, claim, and later action boundaries. This closes the missing consumer-enforcement contract without claiming an unnamed platform already supplies it.

### I3 — ADDRESSED: full rollback inventory and authenticated launcher ABI

**Evidence:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-p1-production-routing-and-worker-fencing-design.md:85`, `:98`–`:99`, `:109`, `:121`; `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-p1-production-recovery-and-alerts-design.md:42`, `:153`, `:168`; `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-p1-production-policy-and-revocation-design.md:183`–`:186`.

The fixture-only two-ID API is no longer the production restart contract. Recovery owns `verify_p1_recovery_rollback_v2`; routing owns its externally pinned, authenticated version-2 launcher ABI. The verifier discovers the complete affected workspace/source/work inventory, with the policy rollback contract explicitly including all operation, permit/claim/pointer, case/work/hold, receipt, attempt, reservation, and liability families. Caller-supplied hold/operation lists cannot narrow the scope.

Both reason predicates execute on every run; zero, one, and many rows are legitimate inventory shapes only after complete discovery and validation. Mixed fixture/production sources, all supported snapshot versions, sorted inventory digests, and pre/post identity/fingerprint equality are required. Unsupported versions, omitted extra rows, incomplete queries, missing snapshots, changed liabilities, or verifier failure deny before exec/registration. Production restart requires an artifact that understands the exact production schema and barriers; a 0043-only artifact does not qualify. Actual-image acceptance now covers these cases.

### I4 — ADDRESSED: legal reason/state predicates for both immutable holds

**Evidence:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-p1-production-recovery-and-alerts-design.md:44`, `:63`, `:69`, `:79`–`:81`, `:159`, `:167`.

Production immutable holds now explicitly support `expired_permit_receipt` on an open cycle and `published_during_cancel` on a closed/cancelled cycle. The expired-permit form may reference an already matching case but does not close a cycle or fabricate a case merely to store immutable evidence. The close-race form does not open or reopen a case.

Both forms retain complete, versioned liability snapshots and original operation identity. The acceptance matrix specifies each legal state/reason pair, rejection of the wrong pair, concurrent exact insertion, real-process crash after commit, one stable hold/work ID, and byte-equivalent liabilities across restart/replay. The previous impossible expired-permit production target is removed.

### I5 — ADDRESSED: operationless pre-admission intent source

**Evidence:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-p1-production-recovery-and-alerts-design.md:58`–`:69`, `:83`, `:91`, `:159`–`:160`, `:169`–`:170`.

The work model now has explicit `cycle_case`, `production_publication_hold`, and `pre_admission_intent_case` source variants. The intent variant binds the canonical intent, eligibility revision, admission disposition, due/expiry deadline, goal/workspace, and owner while requiring NULL cycle/context/operation/remote target. Those NULLs are legal only for that discriminant. It has no provider-read permission and is rejected before provider-client construction.

An authorized typed wake rechecks current eligibility, authority, stop, expiry, and limits. A cycle/context/operation is allocated only by successful canonical admission, while the intent remains stable. The tests cover concurrent adoption/wake, crashes on either side of commit, stale/changed eligibility, revocation, exhausted wake bounds, provider-read rejection, and no premature allocation. The previously unconditional operation requirement is now qualified by the explicit source/nullability contract.

### I6 — ADDRESSED: production case-command and deadline ownership

**Evidence:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-p1-production-recovery-and-alerts-design.md:41`, `:77`, `:85`–`:97`, `:126`, `:164`.

`ProductionRecoveryCaseController` is now an owned internal production boundary. It reuses canonical typed case/admission/command/event/archive records and their revision, transition, owner, deadline, action-limit, terminal-disposition, retention, and idempotency rules. It uses separate production authorization and a restricted role; fixture public commands, account/purpose bindings, wait driver, and notification routes remain disabled in production.

The business-state read worker remains unable to issue commands. The controller owns separately authorized review/resume, deadline suspension/escalation, terminalization, archive, and pre-admission wake/admission paths. Deadline wake identity is deterministic by source/revision/due time/action and is recorded in the production event ledger. It does not introduce a reconciling-to-retry transition or a second case state machine. The former reference to the fixture “current wait contract” is replaced with explicit production deadline-controller ownership.

Acceptance now exercises these commands with fixture routes disabled, production principals and canonical rows, concurrency/crash replay, deadline/action bounds, stop/revocation and route-fence denial, read-worker prohibition, terminal-only archival, and preservation of fixture endpoint denials. The previously unassigned lifecycle integration is now part of the recovery increment rather than an assumed existing production path.

### I7 — ADDRESSED: planned transitions versus subsequent/unplanned drift

**Evidence:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-13-rg0-rg1-external-acceptance-design.md:49`–`:58`, `:62`–`:67`, `:138`, `:143`, `:147`, `:163`–`:168`.

Freshness now distinguishes NO_MUTATION, a predeclared authorized transition, and subsequent/unplanned drift. Planned mutation records pin exact scope, authorization/ticket, expected transition and versions, actor receipt, and independent final readback before execution. A matching planned transition is valid procedure evidence, and its final reviewed state becomes the currentness comparison point. Subsequent drift invalidates affected observations and dependencies; it cannot erase valid evidence that a planned revocation occurred.

NO_MUTATION still requires matching start/end digests. Permanent revocation finishes with the revoked identity still revoked and denial verified. Restoration, when permitted, is a separately declared transition; old credentials or policy versions cannot be restored merely to force digest equality. The checker contract now includes positive planned-revocation evidence, negative unplanned/subsequent revision drift, NO_MUTATION equality, and rejection of restored revoked credentials. The self-invalidating test sequence is resolved.
